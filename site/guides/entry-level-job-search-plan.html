Practical career guide

Your first four weeks of an intentional job search

Published 2 November 2026

The goal is not to apply to hundreds of unverified vacancies. It is to build evidence of fit, select legitimate opportunities, and create a repeatable application process that works for both technical and business roles.

Week 1: Define two realistic target roles

Start with a narrow shortlist: for example, junior data analyst and reporting analyst, or operations coordinator and customer support specialist. Read five original employer descriptions for each target. Separate mandatory requirements from preferred ones; check whether “entry-level” truly permits little professional experience.

Write down recurring skills, typical tasks, hiring locations and work arrangements. A remote label does not necessarily mean international hiring. If a job specifies a country or requires existing work authorization, respect that requirement before applying.

Week 2: Build a proof-based resume and project portfolio

Tailor a master resume into one version for each target role. Put the most relevant education, projects, work examples and tools near the top. For an IT role, demonstrate a working application, bug fix, report, test suite, or data analysis. For a non-IT role, show a process improvement, research summary, customer-handling example or operations dashboard.

Explain actions and outcomes accurately. Do not invent savings, employment dates or certifications. If you have a portfolio, make sure public links open, private data is removed, and you can explain your contribution.

Week 3: Apply with a quality checklist

  • Open the employer's original posting, not a copied vacancy screenshot.
  • Confirm the role title, job reference, location, deadline if shown, and eligibility.
  • Match the top requirements to real resume evidence, then apply through the employer's designated system.
  • Record the original URL, date, resume version, application ID and next action in a private tracker.
  • Do not pay recruitment, placement or “guaranteed interview” fees to unknown parties.

Choose a sustainable number of well-matched applications rather than relying on an arbitrary daily quota. Track what is working, but avoid assuming silence means rejection.

Week 4: Review results and practise interviews

Review the responses you actually received. If no suitable roles were found, adjust geography, seniority, or target titles rather than adding irrelevant applications. Practise a clear explanation of one project, one challenge you solved, and one reason you fit a specific opening.

For technical interviews, demonstrate your reasoning and test work. For business roles, use a simple situation–action–result structure grounded in experience. Prepare thoughtful questions about role expectations, team processes, and employment arrangements.

A reusable private application tracker

Suggested columns

Company · role · country · original posting URL · date checked · application status · work eligibility checked · next follow-up · notes.

Keep this tracker private and store only information you are comfortable retaining. Never publish identification documents, bank details or recruiter conversations in a public portfolio.

Common questions

Should I apply to jobs asking for one or two years of experience?

Read the employer's actual requirements. Relevant internships, coursework or projects may help demonstrate skills, but do not misrepresent your employment history.

Should I use the same resume for IT and non-IT jobs?

A common master resume helps, but tailored versions make the relevant evidence much easier to identify.

What if the posting disappears after I save it?

The employer may have closed or changed the opening. Check the official careers page again instead of relying on an old aggregator link.
